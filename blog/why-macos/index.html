Why macOS? A Personal Take on Apple's Ecosystem

I've been using macOS since 2008. Back then, I spent an entire month's salary on my first Mac. The main reason? A former physics teacher of mine said that Apple products offer the best ecosystem. I knew the name Apple, and it was the era when the first iPhone was launching, but I had never really had any hands-on experience. I found the idea creative and aesthetically very appealing—just having an all-in-one Mac device. Since then, I've never looked back. I can't imagine a better system. In this post, I want to explain why macOS is my first choice and why I find it so difficult to use anything else.

The Trackpad

I had used Linux systems before and even tried to build a layout similar to a Mac. But there are certain advantages and quirks I simply don't find anywhere else—which makes it virtually impossible for me to switch. First and foremost: the trackpad. It might seem inconspicuous, but it makes the biggest difference for me. The trackpad offers so many functions and gestures that allow for incredibly rich interaction. For me, that's the essence—the communication between human and machine. It's an absolutely unique quality that no other system offers. Neither Windows nor Linux comes even close to such pleasant, intelligent interaction. One feature in particular—which Apple disables by default and even hides in the settings—is the ability to move windows via drag-and-drop using three fingers. For me, it's revolutionary. There are third-party solutions for Windows that try to emulate the trackpad, but it just doesn't work as organically or naturally. That alone is reason enough for me to say I could never use another system. But it's just one essential part of why I chose macOS.

File Management

Another extremely important aspect—though the systems have become more similar over time—is file management. I absolutely hate that in Windows, when a file is open or in use, you can't rename it or change its location. Windows practically forces you to close the application completely before you can rename or move the file. In daily workflow, it often happens that you need to quickly rename a folder or perform similar interactions, and Windows makes this completely impossible. I can never understand why this hasn't been fixed—it would be technically trivial today. On macOS, you have the freedom to rename, change paths, or reorganize folders at any time, even while files are open. No problem at all.

The Golden Cage

When it comes to interaction between Apple devices, it's clear: you live in a "golden cage" where devices communicate extremely well with each other. You can move data back and forth quickly. There are now many third-party systems that enable communication between Android and macOS, and AirDrop is sometimes supported. But the fact is: interaction within the Apple ecosystem is simply magical. Whether it's AirPods that automatically switch from your iPhone to your Mac when you start a video, or having a MacBook next to you and controlling it with your mouse—it all works so organically and naturally together. It makes for an extremely powerful tool for productive work, something you won't find even remotely in other systems. There are many competitors like Huawei and others that offer their own ecosystems, but it just doesn't work like Apple's. Time will tell if others catch up, but currently Apple simply offers the best ecosystem by far.

The Small Ideas

Last but not least: I could bring up many more arguments, but these are the most important points for me personally. And then there's another essential aspect. It's not about a single main feature where you say, "That's the reason." It's above all the many small ideas behind it—the many small considerations Apple has made. Whether it's the ability to perform calculations—multiplication, addition—right in Spotlight or Notes without opening a calculator, or the many shortcuts hidden throughout the system. It's these small daily interactions that don't make the big difference on their own, but they foster a certain way of thinking, a kind of intuition that is simply unique, logical, and in my eyes, intelligently solved. This multitude of small ideas and intuitions is the big picture that makes it impossible for me to use other systems. I've tried to switch so many times, to try something new, and I'm genuinely open. But you always come back to this golden cage. At this point, it's truly the gold standard.

Why macOS?

So, why macOS? Because it's not just an operating system—it's a philosophy of thoughtful design, seamless integration, and intuitive interaction. And once you've experienced it, it's hard to go back.
